Vibrant Matter: The Political Ecology of Things

“Sullivan reminds us that a vital materiality can never really be thrown "away," for it continues its activities even as a discarded or unwanted
commodity. For Sullivan that day, as for me on that June morning, thing power rose from a pile of trash.” chapter 1 p6
    
    Much like Sullivan and Bennett, the stacks and stacks of empty and repurposed cardboard boxes slowly taking over the house I’ve lived in for my whole life suddenly called to me as I began to pay more attention to things as things rather than mere objects
    
When I stop to look at the overwhelming amount of boxes, I am overcome with a harrowing sense emptiness. I feel the failed attempts at filling the void through mindless consumerism, only for the things these boxes formerly housed to be forgotten because the person who bought them held no affection for it in the first place. I spent a good portion of my childhood terrified of stepping foot into the basement. It was dark, cold, and simply an unnavigable maze of abandoned things, ever growing and only contained by my mother organizing and stacking boxes to make pathways for us to walk through. Each time a pathway got blocked, she would create a new, slimmer path.
